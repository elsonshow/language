Tenses

English has 12 main tenses, each used to express different time frames and aspects. Understanding tenses is crucial for clear communication.

Present Simple

Used for habits, routines, general truths, and permanent situations.

  • She works in a hospital.
  • The sun rises in the east.
  • We play football every Saturday.

Past Simple

Used for completed actions in the past.

  • They visited Paris last summer.
  • I finished my homework yesterday.
  • She lived in London for five years.

Future Simple

Used for predictions, promises, and future plans.

  • We will go to the movies tomorrow.
  • She promises she'll call you.
  • It will rain next week.

Passive Voice

The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action rather than the doer of the action.

Structure: be + past participle

Examples

  • Active: The chef cooks the meal.
  • Passive: The meal is cooked by the chef.
  • Active: They built the house in 2005.
  • Passive: The house was built in 2005.
  • Active: She will write the report.
  • Passive: The report will be written by her.

Articles

Articles (a, an, the) are used to specify nouns. They help identify whether we're talking about a specific or general item.

Definite Article (the)

Used for specific nouns that both the speaker and listener know about.

  • The sun is shining today.
  • Can you pass the salt?
  • I visited the Eiffel Tower last year.

Indefinite Articles (a, an)

Used for general, non-specific nouns.

  • I saw a cat in the garden.
  • She's an excellent teacher.
  • Would you like a cup of tea?

Modals

Modal verbs are used to express ability, permission, obligation, possibility, and more.

Common Modal Verbs

  • Can/Could: Ability, possibility
  • May/Might: Permission, possibility
  • Must/Have to: Obligation
  • Should: Advice, recommendation
  • Would: Polite requests, preferences

Examples

  • I can speak Spanish fluently.
  • You should eat more vegetables.
  • May I borrow your pen?
  • We must finish this project by Friday.

Conditionals

Conditionals are used to talk about hypothetical situations and their consequences.

Zero Conditional

Used for general truths and scientific facts.

  • If it rains, the ground gets wet.
  • If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.

First Conditional

Used for real or probable future situations.

  • If it rains tomorrow, we'll stay at home.
  • If you study hard, you'll pass the exam.

Second Conditional

Used for unreal or imaginary present/future situations.

  • If I had a million dollars, I'd buy a house.
  • If I were you, I'd apologize.

Third Conditional

Used for unreal past situations.

  • If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.
  • If she had left earlier, she wouldn't have missed the bus.
